# The brief

The brief is the film before any drawing: what it's about, the world it happens in, what's always true, how it
should look, and where the song turns. Every later choice gets checked against it, and the review pages quote it.
Write it with the person, in their words. Ten good minutes here saves hours of renders.

## Ask these, one or two at a time

1. **What's the song about, and how should the video feel?** Get one sentence of intent. Good ones name a feeling
   with a tension in it ("a hometown that makes you feel sick to your stomach and at home at the same time"), not a
   genre ("dark and moody").
2. **Where does it happen?** A real place is a gift: a street, a lake, a parking lot, a bedroom. What's in it, what
   time of day, what weather, what season. Is anything named on screen, or never named?
3. **What's in every frame, and what's never in any frame?** These become the rules. Examples: no faces, ever; one
   steady warm light and nothing else warm; a figure seen only from behind; nothing readable (no signs, no plates);
   frame one moves even with the sound off.
4. **What should it look made of?** Charcoal, pencil, paint, film, paper, chalk. References: painters, films,
   photographs, album art. If there's an album cover, the first frame can be the cover, alive.
5. **Where does the song turn?** Listen with them, or read the cue sheet from `tools/analyze.py` together: where
   things come in, drop out, build, stop. Each turn gets a picture.
6. **Which aspects, and where does it go?** 16:9 for YouTube; 9:16 for Shorts, Reels and TikTok. Plan both from the
   start: layouts are per aspect, and a vertical that's a crop of the wide one never works.
7. **Words?** Titles, captions, an end card. Whose voice they're in.

## Turn the answers into BRIEF.md

- **Intent:** the one sentence.
- **Place and world:** the specifics. Name the objects, they become assets to draw.
- **Rules:** the list from question 3, plus "a pure function of time".
- **Look:** references and palette; the media it's made in.
- **Cue sheet:** time, what the music does, what the picture does. Measured times only; rough ones say "section".
- **Key frames (Gate A):** four to seven times that, as stills, prove the film: frame one, the biggest moment, the
  turn, the ending. These are drawn and reviewed before anything is rendered.
- **Preview (Gate B):** one 10 to 15 second window across the hardest change (a drop, a stop, a build).
- **Acceptance:** reads at phone size, holds in 9:16, nothing important cut off, no flashes, the rules all hold.

## A cue sheet that works

| Time | Music | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00-0:39 | pads; low swells at 0:06, 0:17, 0:28 | the cover, still, snowing; each swell is a gust that shows one far thing in the fog |
| 0:39 | bass in | the first step |
| 1:27 | hats in | the snow turns sideways, the figure leans into it |
| 2:11.25 | hats out (the first missing hat) | the wind dies and every flake stops in the air |

Note the precision where it matters (2:11.25) and the plain description of what changes. "Things get intense" isn't a
cue; "the snow turns sideways" is.

## Good habits

- One idea per section of the song. If a section has two ideas, one of them is for another song.
- Decide what the light does. Most scenes are about one light.
- Plan the ending: the last 10 seconds are what people remember, and the end card needs room.
- Write down what you're not doing. It stops scope creep at Gate B.
